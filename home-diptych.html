Earlier this month, the Journal of Young Investigators (JYI) executive board came together in Seattle for our annual in-person summit! This was my second summit, and as the 22nd Editor-in-Chief, it is always rewarding to step back and look at the whole journal at once. We spent Saturday going through every department’s year in review, the challenges ahead, and the plans to tackle them. Near the end of the day, we went through who is graduating this spring, which is a big part of running a journal that is entirely student-led. That turnover is something our team has thought about a lot. Back in April at the Council on Undergraduate Research's NCUR, we presented a poster on what we called “knowledge drain”: when experienced members graduate, much of what they know leaves with them. Our approach has been to build structure into the journal, with a multi-stage hiring process, asynchronous onboarding that works across time zones, tiered training, and a shared workflow board for every manuscript. This year put that system to the test, and I’m proud of how the team grew: - 👥 Staff Growth: 27 new members joined from 96 applications - 🔬 Higher Standards: every manuscript now goes to two associate editors and a research editor, with a two-week review window, and we published an AI policy for both authors and reviewers - 🌍 Increased Access: we reestablished our translations department, brought back Perspective articles, and are developing partnerships for undergraduate research events In 2027, JYI turns 30! I look forward to seeing where we go in the next year A massive thank you to the editorial board for everything they carry: - Hallel Abrams Gerber, Managing Editor - Julian Lau, Senior Research Editor - Manjushree Balaji, Senior News and Careers Editor - Jinglan Liang, Translations Coordinator Thank you as well to Amrutha Alibilli, and the rest of the corporate board (including the famous Cindy Wu), and to Nafisa Jadavji, PhD, FAHA, and the Board of Directors for their continued guidance. On a lighter note, the very last item on our agenda was cleaning the Airbnb exactly to the host’s instructions. Thankfully, a house full of editors knows how to follow a style guide! 🧹 #JYI #UndergraduateResearch #PeerReview #ScienceCommunication #StudentLeadership

Why molecules and manuscripts

Molecules and manuscripts are two different interests, but they are two sides of one coin, and that coin is who I am. University is meant to teach you how to think, and each turban I wear teaches me a different way.

Molecules

I stumbled into research. It started as a box to check on the way to medicine. In my first semester I joined a peptide lab as a math major, surrounded by chemists, with no clue what I was getting myself into. I took it seriously anyway, and it became the career I want.

Not everything goes to plan. When our lab moved to Houston, our team kept the work going from Newark.

On the ambulance, medicine is about the next ten minutes. In the lab, it is about the next ten years. Translational medicine sits between the two, and that is where I want to work: treating patients, and helping build treatments that reach far more than one person.

Manuscripts

I stumbled into history too. While studying abroad in Sweden, I met Satnam Singh, a Danish historian. I read his work and wanted to add to the field myself.

The tools are getting extraordinary. Soon we will design molecules faster than we can test them, and the hardest questions will not be computational: who gets these therapies, who patients trust, how we care for people at the end of life. History helps me think about those.

Sikh history stays with me too, as a pursuit of its own.
