Research Experience

  • Computational and Mathematical Biology
  • Oct 2023 – PresentUndergraduate Researcher and Computational Biology Team Lead KumarLab; PI: Dr Vivek Kumar, University of Houston (previously NJIT)
    • Lead undergraduate and graduate trainees in computational development of peptide therapeutics; trained 15+ lab associates and secured over $15,000 in direct project funding.
    • Designed cyclic peptides against multiple sclerosis autoantibodies; ran molecular-dynamics simulations for the published lead peptide CAP8 (KPYPFWDD), from an eight-residue design scaffold and Rosetta/FlexPepDock workflow.
    • Validated designs with 100 ns GROMACS simulations and alanine scanning; in the published study, CAP8 bound anti-GlialCAM autoantibody with Kd = 0.294 mM, versus 4.82 mM linear and 2.61 mM unrelated cyclic controls, and cut epitope detection on Western blot by 69% versus 27% and 5% for controls.
    • Led a funded non-hormonal male-contraception project: machine-learning-guided cyclic peptide design against redacted target, docking and refinement; grant writing, manuscript and patent application in preparation.
    • Conducted dissections and assisted animal work on angiogenic hydrogels subsequently published; maintained computational research after the lab relocated to Houston.
  • Jun 2026 – PresentUndergraduate Researcher, Computational Bionanotechnology PI: Prof Stefano Angioletti-Uberti, Department of Materials, Imperial College London
    • Apply statistical mechanics, molecular dynamics, protein language models and machine-learning methods to enzyme and binder design and soft-matter/biomaterial systems.
    • Contributed code to BAGEL (Biomolecular Algorithm for Guidance in Energy Landscape), a tool for searching biomolecular energy landscapes; investigate peptide designs that mimic natural ligands.
  • Sep 2026 – PresentUndergraduate Researcher, Systems Biology PI: Dr Horacio Rotstein, NJIT
    • Simplify and model the MAPK/ERK (Ras-Raf-MEK-ERK) signaling pathway in cancer as a dynamical system, to identify points for therapeutic intervention.
  • Oct 2025 – May 2026Undergraduate Researcher, Mathematical Modeling PI: Dr James Maclaurin, NJIT
    • Applied Hawkes self-exciting point processes to secondary nucleation and feedback in amyloid formation.
  • Intellectual and Cultural History
  • Sep 2026 – Dec 2026Emerging Scholars Research Fellow, Early Modern Sikh History Harvard Sikh Center; mentor: Satnam Singh
    • Research ‘Dissemination and the making of Khalsa authority in a contested panth’: how the early Khalsa came to dominate the wider Sikh world against rival claimants, including the Bandai Khalsa, Minas, Ram Raiyas and Hindaliyas.
    • Argue that the answer lies less in politics than in transmission: the Khalsa’s consolidation of ‘authentic Sikh teachings’ through katha preceded and enabled its control of the panth.
    • Sources: Gurmukhi and Persian primary materials, including works attributed to Bhai Mani Singh, hukamname, rahit-nama and sampardaic literature, alongside Persian and European accounts of the period.
    • Twelve-week fellowship term producing a paper and a presentation: first, a characterization of the intellectual landscape and its conflicts; second, a prosopography linking intellectual influences to the period’s political figures.
  • Sep 2026 – PresentUndergraduate Researcher, History of Medicine and Bioethics PI: Dr Stephen Pemberton, NJIT
    • Trace the legal history of end-of-life decision-making from In re Quinlan (1976) to Cruzan v. Director, Missouri Department of Health (1990).
    • Compare how three physician-authors, Sherwin Nuland, Atul Gawande and Haider Warraich, account for dying in modern American medicine; develop scholarship intended for submission to a peer-reviewed humanities journal.
  • Jan 2024 – May 2024, Jan 2026 – May 2026Undergraduate Researcher, Digital History and Urban Studies PI: Dr Louis Hamilton, NJIT; field research in Rome and Florence
    • Two solo-authored geospatial narratives (StoryMapJS) mapping sites visited on foot against historical and archival sources.
    • Power vs. the People (HIST 328): mapped 18 sites, layering Counter-Reformation Marian shrines against Julius II’s 1508 papal axis along the Via Giulia, Rome, drawing on the Early Modern Rome Reconstructed database and Rufini’s 1853 shrine census; argued the street shows superimposition and negotiation rather than a papal-versus-popular divide.
    • Processions and Power (HIST 329): mapped 22 Florentine sites to examine how urban expansion transformed liturgical processions into contests of ecclesiastical and household prestige, read against Dante’s critique of the gente nuova.
