Mapping religion and the medieval Italian city

NJIT · PI: Dr. Louis Hamilton · field research in Rome and Florence

Question
How did Christian institutions shape the built form and civic life of medieval and early modern Italian cities — and does the usual story of top-down religious authority actually fit the evidence on the ground?
Approach
Two solo-authored geospatial narratives, each mapping sites visited on foot against historical and archival sources.

Power vs. the People — the Via Giulia, Rome

18 mapped sites. Layers the Counter-Reformation Marian street shrines against Julius II's 1508 papal axis, drawing on the Early Modern Rome Reconstructed database and Alessandro Rufini's 1853 shrine census. The argument: the street sustains two devotional regimes decades apart, and the conventional papal-versus-popular dichotomy misdescribes what is actually superimposition and continuous negotiation.

Processions and Power — Florence

22 mapped sites. Traces how Florence's demographic and territorial expansion turned liturgical processions from instruments of civic cohesion into contested displays of ecclesiastical and household prestige, reading Dante's critique of the gente nuova against the city's shifting processional geography.
