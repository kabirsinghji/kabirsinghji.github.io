How American medicine learned to talk about dying

NJIT · PI: Dr. Stephen Pemberton · 2026–present

Question
How did legal, clinical, and cultural developments from the late twentieth century onward reshape American end-of-life care?
The courts
From In re Quinlan (1976) to Cruzan (1990): how courts came to decide who may stop treatment for a patient who can no longer speak, and where they drew the line between patient autonomy and the limits of medical intervention. Quinlan was decided here in New Jersey.
The physicians
A comparative reading of three doctors writing about death from inside medicine: Sherwin Nuland’s How We Die (1994), Atul Gawande’s Being Mortal (2014), and Haider Warraich’s Modern Death (2017). All three agree that medicine has made dying harder; the paper reads them against each other for what each thinks went wrong.
Where it stands
Independent study, fall 2026, developing original scholarship for submission to a peer-reviewed humanities journal.
