Scientific Research

Cyclic peptides that block fertilization

KumarLab · PI: Dr. Vivek Kumar · 2023–present

Question
Can a cyclic peptide be designed to interrupt sperm–egg recognition selectively enough to work as a non-hormonal male contraceptive?
Approach
Machine-learning-guided design of cyclic peptides against sperm-specific protein targets, followed by docking and refinement.
Where it stands
Manuscript and patent application both in preparation. Presented at the AAP/ASCI/APSA Joint Meeting and the National Contraception Meeting; took first place at the NJIT Honors Interdisciplinary Research Forum.

A cyclic peptide for multiple sclerosis

KumarLab · PI: Dr. Vivek Kumar · published 2026

Question
MS therapies mostly suppress whole B- and T-cell populations, which works but carries serious infection and malignancy risk. Could you instead block just the autoantibodies doing the damage — and with a single peptide, rather than one per target?
Approach
Rational in silico design against the CDRL3 loops of 23 human MS autoantibodies. CDRL3 loops are structurally homogeneous where CDRH3 loops are not, so one 8-mer scaffold can plausibly fit many targets. Rosetta design and FlexPepDock produced CAP8 (KPYPFWDD), validated by 100 ns GROMACS simulations and alanine scanning.
Result
CAP8 bound anti-GlialCAM autoantibody with a Kd of 0.294 mM, roughly an order of magnitude tighter than linear or scrambled cyclic controls, and cut autoantibody detection of its epitope by 69% on Western blot. Binding to a non-pathological influenza antibody was indistinguishable from controls, which is the selectivity the approach needs.
Where it stands
Published in European Journal of Medicinal Chemistry. Inhibition is partial, not complete, and control peptides showed modest effects of their own — selectivity needs firmer evidence before in vivo work. Read the paper →

BAGEL — searching biomolecular energy landscapes

Imperial College London · PI: Prof. Stefano Angioletti-Uberti · 2026–present

Question
How do you guide a search through an energy landscape toward sequences that fold and bind the way you intended?
Approach
Statistical mechanics, molecular dynamics, and protein language models applied to enzyme and binder design.

Amyloid aggregation as a self-exciting process

NJIT · PI: Dr. James Maclaurin · 2025–2026

Question
Secondary nucleation makes amyloid growth accelerate itself. Can that feedback be captured by a point process instead of a rate equation?
Approach
Hawkes self-exciting point processes applied to nucleation dynamics in amyloid formation.
